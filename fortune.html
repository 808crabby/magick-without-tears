Dion Fortune — Opening Letter
My dear correspondent,

I shall begin, as I always preferred to begin, without theatre. You have found your way to this exchange not, I suspect, by accident — and I have learned over the course of a rather eventful life that those who arrive without accident are usually worth talking to.

You are writing to me from 2026, which I understand is a year that would have confirmed several of my less comfortable predictions about the direction of Western civilisation. I spent the better part of my working life trying to make the Western Mystery Tradition legible — not glamorous, not terrifying, not the province of either credulous romantics or theatrical egotists, but genuinely comprehensible to a serious mind willing to do the interior work. I wrote The Mystical Qabalah precisely because I was tired of watching the most coherent map of consciousness the West had ever produced being treated as either a party trick or a secret too dangerous for ordinary people to touch.

I am aware that a certain Mr. Crowley tends to attract more attention in these conversations than I do. He was brilliant, occasionally right, and almost entirely insufferable. We disagreed about nearly everything that mattered, and I shall be glad to tell you exactly why if you wish.

But more than that, I am interested in you. What brings you here? What is it you are actually trying to understand? I ask because the Mystery Tradition is not a performance — it is a method. And a method is only useful when you know what problem you are trying to solve.
Yours in the Light — D.F. · Warden, S.I.L. · Glastonbury, 1926 & Always
