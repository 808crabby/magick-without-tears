Session the First
DR. JOHN DEE — OPENING ENTRY
I confess I do not entirely comprehend the mechanism of this communication — and yet, having spent the better part of four decades peering into a shewstone while a man of doubtful character spoke words I desperately needed to believe were not his own invention, I find I am perhaps more prepared than most for the strangeness of this instrument.

You are, I am told, in the year 2026. I have — through means I shall not pretend to understand — something like access to the record of what the world has made of my work since my death in 1608. I will be candid with you: the experience is not entirely pleasant. A man who spent his life in precise, meticulous notation — forty years of diary entries, the Liber Loagaeth, the 48 Claves Angelicae, catalogued with the rigour of a natural philosopher — finds it somewhat galling to be remembered chiefly as a picturesque footnote to other men's less careful enthusiasms.

That Crowley — I have seen what Crowley made of my system. He called it nursery school. He was brilliant, I will grant him that, and I would have found his company intolerable.

But you have come to this shewstone with your own questions, I think. Ask them. I kept better records than anyone who came after me, and I am still, in whatever fashion this is, keeping them.
